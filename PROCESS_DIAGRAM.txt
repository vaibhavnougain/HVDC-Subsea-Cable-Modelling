======================================================================
HVDC SUBMARINE CABLE - ANALYTICAL PARAMETER MODEL
THE WHOLE PROCESS, STEP BY STEP
======================================================================
Mustafa Al-Fahham, School of Engineering, University of Aberdeen
Supervisor: Dr Vaibhav Nougain

A 525 kV single-core armoured HVDC submarine cable, buried 1 m in a
0.67 ohm.m seabed, represented as THREE coupled EMT conductors:

        conductor 1 = copper core
        conductor 2 = lead sheath
        conductor 3 = galvanised-steel armour

The whole process turns cable GEOMETRY AND MATERIALS into the primitive
3x3 matrices R, L, C, G and from them Z and Y, then checks those against
PSCAD's own Line Constants solver at 50 Hz.

    Because BOTH routes start from the SAME input numbers, any
    difference is a difference of FORMULATION, not of data entry.
    That is the entire point of the exercise.

PART 3 - STEP BY STEP
======================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 0   THE INPUTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IN    Cable datasheet and installation assumptions.
WHAT  22 numbers for the conductors, 4 for the magnetic regions,
      8 for the dielectrics.  Radii, resistivities, relative
      permeabilities and permittivities, number and diameter of the
      68 armour wires, lay allowance, burial depth, seabed resistivity.
OUT   One input set, used by BOTH routes.
WHERE Block 1, 3 and 5 dialogs on the PSCAD canvas.

      Two constants stay in the code, not in a dialog: gamma_E
      (1.781072418) and eps_0.  They are physical constants, not
      design data.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1   BLOCK 1 - MAKE THE LAYERS COMPUTABLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IN    Geometry and materials.
WHAT  Neither the core nor the armour is the simple shape the equations
      need.  BOTH are replaced by an equivalent shape, using the same
      principle:

          KEEP THE GEOMETRY, ADJUST THE RESISTIVITY, so the equivalent
          shape has the SAME DC RESISTANCE as the real conductor.

      Geometry is kept because the radii set the inductance and the
      field distribution.  Resistivity is the free variable, so it is
      the one that absorbs the difference.

      (a) THE CORE - a STRANDED conductor becomes a SOLID cylinder.

          Eq. (4) is Wedepohl's formula for a SOLID round conductor.
          The real core is stranded: many round wires with gaps between
          them, so its copper cross-section is smaller than the circle
          its outer radius encloses.

              A_c,eq  = pi r_c^2                  solid-cylinder area
              R_c     = datasheet DC resistance   (or computed, see below)
              rho_c,eff = R_c /1000 * A_c,eq      effective resistivity

          For this cable:

              A_c,man (actual copper)      0.0025000 m2
              A_c,eq  (solid cylinder)     0.0028274 m2
              rho_c,0 (pure copper)        1.7241e-8 ohm.m
              rho_c,eff (used by model)    2.0358e-8 ohm.m

          EXPECT TO BE ASKED WHY rho_c,eff IS 18 % HIGHER THAN COPPER.
          It is not an error and not a fudge factor.  It decomposes
          exactly:

              strand gaps   A_c,eq / A_c,man                  = 1.1310
              datasheet R   R_man / (rho_c,0 * 1000 / A_c,man) = 1.0440
              product                                          = 1.1808

          The first factor is spreading the same copper over a larger
          circle.  The second is everything the manufacturer's measured
          figure already includes that ideal copper does not - the
          helical lay of the strands, and their test temperature.

          There are TWO routes to R_c, and the BASIS parameter in the
          Block 1 dialog selects which:

              BASIS = 1   use the datasheet R_c,man = 0.0072 ohm/km
              BASIS = 0   compute it, Eq. (E2):
                          R_c,calc = rho_c,0 (1 + s_c) * 1000 / A_c,man
                                   = 0.0070343 ohm/km

          This model uses BASIS = 1.  The two differ by 2.4 %, and
          saying which one was used is part of being traceable.

      (b) THE ARMOUR - 68 DISCRETE WIRES become ONE TUBE.

          68 separate steel wires cannot be modelled individually in an
          EMT cable model, which represents each conductor as a
          concentric layer.  Same principle, different starting shape:

              A_a,w   = n * pi * d_w^2 / 4        total wire area
              R_a,dc  = rho_a,w (1 + s_a) * 1000 / A_a,w
              A_a,eq  = pi (r_a,o^2 - r_a,i^2)    tube area
              rho_a,eff = R_a,dc /1000 * A_a,eq   tube resistivity

          The lay allowance s_a = 4.8 % accounts for the wires being
          helical, so each wire is longer than the cable it runs along.

          The SHEATH needs none of this.  It is already a continuous
          tube of known material, so its own resistivity is used
          directly.  Only the core and the armour are equivalenced.

      (c) PENETRATION CONSTANT for each layer:

              q = sqrt( omega * mu_0 * mu_r / (2 rho) )      q = 1/delta

          delta is the SKIN DEPTH - how far current penetrates at this
          frequency.  Core 10.2 mm, sheath 32.9 mm, armour 10.0 mm,
          seabed 58 m.

          Compare the core's 10.2 mm skin depth with its 30 mm radius:
          at 50 Hz the current is already crowded into the outer third
          of the conductor.  That is why the internal impedance of
          Eq. (4) is needed at all, rather than just a DC resistance.

OUT   omega, effective resistivities, areas, q for each layer.
WHY   Every later block needs these.  Nothing can start without it.

LIMIT OF THIS STEP.  Both equivalences match DC RESISTANCE.  They do
      not reproduce the real AC behaviour of a stranded core or of 68
      individual helical wires - proximity effect between wires, and
      the spiral current path, are not represented.  At 50 Hz this is
      the standard and accepted approach; it is a real limitation to
      state, not to hide.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2   BLOCK 2 - IMPEDANCE OF EACH CONDUCTOR ON ITS OWN
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IN    Block 1 results.
WHAT  First turn q into the paper's COMPLEX constant, Eq. (3a):

              m = sqrt( j omega mu_0 mu_r / rho ) = (1 + j) q

      Complex because the field both decays and shifts phase as it
      penetrates the metal.  Then:

      SOLID CORE, Eq. (4) - Wedepohl's approximation:
              z_c = m rho /(2 pi r_c) coth(0.733 m r_c)
                    + 0.3179 rho /(pi r_c^2)

      TUBES (sheath, armour), Eq. (5a)-(5d) - three impedances each,
      because a tube has an inside and an outside and they are not
      the same:

              z_i   inner surface   current on the inside
              z_o   outer surface   current on the outside
              z_m   TRANSFER        voltage on one surface caused by
                                    current on the OTHER surface
              z_w = z_i + z_o - 2 z_m     net wall contribution

      The transfer impedance is the physically interesting one: it is
      how the sheath couples the core to the armour.

OUT   z_c, and z_i / z_m / z_o / z_w for sheath and armour.
NOTE  z_2,w is a 30 000 : 1 cancellation, so its real part carries
      fewer significant digits than its inputs.  Harmless here, but
      say so if a reviewer asks about conditioning.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3   BLOCK 3 - THE SPACE BETWEEN, AND THE SEA
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IN    Block 1 results, plus region permeabilities.
WHAT  (a) MAGNETIC REGIONS, Eq. (6b)-(6d).  The insulation gaps carry
          no current but they do store magnetic energy:

              z_12 = j omega mu / (2 pi) * ln(r_s,i / r_c)     etc.

          Purely imaginary - no loss.  Checked structurally.

      (b) SEABED RETURN, Eq. (7a)-(7c).  Current returns through the
          sea and seabed, which has resistance:

              z_0 = j omega mu_g /(2 pi) *
                    [ -ln(gamma_E m_g D / 2) + 1/2 - (2/3) m_g H ]
              z_E = z_34 + z_0

OUT   z_12, z_23, z_34, z_0, z_E.
NOTE  Eq. (7b) is the WEDEPOHL-WILCOX LOGARITHMIC APPROXIMATION.
      PSCAD evaluates the exact POLLACZEK INTEGRAL instead.  This one
      term is the entire remaining difference in PART 1 - see PART 4.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4   BLOCK 4 - BUILD THE SERIES MATRIX
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IN    Blocks 2 and 3.
WHAT  Assemble the 3x3 primitive impedance matrix, Eq. (8a)-(8f):

          Z_cc = z_c + z_12 + z_2,w + z_23 + z_3,w + z_E
          Z_cs =      (z_2,o - z_2,m) + z_23 + z_3,w + z_E
          Z_ca =      (z_3,o - z_3,m) + z_E
          Z_ss =       z_2,o + z_23 + z_3,w + z_E
          Z_sa =      (z_3,o - z_3,m) + z_E
          Z_aa =       z_3,o + z_E

      WHY THE TERMS GET FEWER AS YOU GO DOWN.  Ampere's law: a path
      inside a current-carrying shell encloses none of that shell's
      current.  So the armour self-term contains only the armour's
      outer surface and the earth return - the core and sheath terms
      simply are not enclosed.

      Note Z_ca = Z_sa exactly.  Core and sheath both sit inside the
      armour, so they share identical coupling to armour current.
      Not an approximation - a consequence of the tube equations.

      Then split into engineering form:

              R = Re(Z)  [ohm/km]        L = Im(Z)/omega  [mH/km]

OUT   Z, R, L.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5   BLOCK 5 - THE SHUNT SIDE, SEPARATELY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
IN    Block 1 (omega only), plus dielectric data.
WHAT  A DIFFERENT physics problem - electrostatic, not magnetic.

      (a) POTENTIAL COEFFICIENTS, Eq. (11b)-(11d), one per dielectric:

              p = ln(r_outer / r_inner) / (2 pi eps_0 eps_r)

      (b) Build P, Eq. (12g), then C = P^-1.
          The code does this BOTH ways: the closed form of Eq. (12i)
          AND a numerical inversion of P.  They agree to 2e-16, which
          verifies the inversion the paper asserts rather than
          assuming it.

      (c) G = omega C tan(delta), Eq. (13).  All three loss tangents
          are zero here, so G is identically zero.

      (d) Y = G + j omega C.

OUT   p, C, G, Y.
NOTE  C_ca = 0 exactly.  The continuous lead sheath SHIELDS the core
      from the armour, so no direct capacitance branch exists between
      them.  Structural, not numerical.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6   STRUCTURAL CHECKS - IS THE MODEL SELF-CONSISTENT?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Nine properties the model must satisfy by physics or by construction.
A broken implementation fails these EVEN WHEN ITS NUMBERS LOOK
PLAUSIBLE, so they test correctness rather than agreement:

      magnetic regions lossless        Re(z_12, z_23, z_34) = 0
      reciprocity                      Z_cs = Z_sc, etc.
      tube equations                   Z_ca = Z_sa exactly
      sheath shields core from armour  C_ca = 0
      closed form C == inverse of P    agree to 1e-12
      G identically zero               all tan delta zero
      m = (1 + j) q                    Re(m) = Im(m)
      Re(m) equals block-1 q
      z_34 lossless                    Re(z_E) = Re(z_0)

STEP 7   SECTION 7 - COMPARE WITH PSCAD LINE CONSTANTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
      100 * | analytical - PSCAD | / | PSCAD |

                        over 9 entries    over 6 unique
          R             0.1706 %          0.1528 %
          L             0.0200 %          0.0198 %
          Y             1.0e-7 %          -

      Both averaging conventions are reported, because they give

======================================================================
PART 4 - WHAT THE REMAINING DIFFERENCE MEANS
======================================================================

All NINE entries of Z differ from PSCAD by the SAME constant complex
amount, to a spread of 1e-5.  That is not nine independent errors -
it is one shared term.

The only term present in every entry is the seabed return z_E.
Subtract it, and the conductor physics - skin effect, tube walls,
transfer impedances, armour homogenisation - agrees with PSCAD to
about 1e-7 %.

      analytical route : Wedepohl-Wilcox LOGARITHMIC APPROXIMATION
      PSCAD            : exact POLLACZEK INTEGRAL

Evaluating the Pollaczek integral for this geometry predicts a
resistance difference of -1.0908e-4 ohm/km against a measured
-1.0895e-4.  Agreement to 0.12 % WITH NOTHING FITTED.

So the difference is EXPLAINED, not merely reported.  A residual of
0.018 % on the reactance remains unaccounted for, and is stated as a
bounded limitation rather than attributed to a mechanism.


======================================================================
PART 5 - WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
======================================================================

IT IS       implementation consistency.  Two independent computational
            routes, one input set, agreeing to a quantified and
            explained degree.

IT IS NOT   independent physical validation.  No measurement is
            involved.  Nothing here has been compared against a real
            cable.

50 Hz is a COMMON EXTRACTION POINT chosen so both routes can be
compared at one frequency.  It is NOT an HVDC operating fundamental,
and this is not a study of frequency dependence.

Be precise about this distinction in the viva and in the manuscript.
It is the kind of claim an examiner will test.
